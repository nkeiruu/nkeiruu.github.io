The semester starts in Octber in Germany so you get to enjoy summer till its very end. This semester, I am taking 5 classes and working part time.  Here are some of my highlights fom first three weeks of my emester:

1. Coaxial Resonator Lab
This really put things like TE modes and impedance matching in context for me. And my goodness, I finally have an intuition for what bandpass filter and inductive coupling are. If you are like me, they are always words floating around that people just use but you can never get a satisfyig definition for. Bandpass filter = frequencies within a certain frequency will get through. The etzmologz is reted to high and low pass filters. Finally, our professor made the connection between the clunky space fiction cylinder (pictured below) and the antennas that allows us to connect to 2.2GHz wifi.

2. Beam Current Measurements Lecture
When I worked as an operator, there was an oscilloscope of truth that let us know whether we were in business or not. The pictured oscilloscope shows beam bunches in Cornell Electron Storage Ring.
![cesrcurrent.jpg](Oscilloscope showing bunches in CESR)

 This week, I learned about the mechanism and devices to measure beam currents like this. You can either directly measure:
    a- its electrical charge (Faraday cup)
    b- its magnetic fields (transformers)
    c- its energy loss in matter (scintillators)

3. MYRRHA Project Overview
I got a cool overview of this project from a professor directly working on it. [MYRRHA](https://www.sckcen.be/en/infrastructure/myrrha) sounds like science fiction --using a proton accelerator for transmutation of nuclear waste-- but I can assure you its real. What is even more unbelieveably cool is its operational reliability requirements: It must run for a period of 250 hrs without any problems!
